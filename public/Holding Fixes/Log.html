Executive Summary & Scope Overview

Following the physical damage assessment across the road network, a series of immediate holding actions have been identified to stabilize surfaces, manage water run-off, and prevent further deterioration ahead of major capital road upgrades. These cataloged inspection points cover interim interventions—including cold-tar pothole patching, crack sealing with emulsion, localized crusher-cement stabilization, slurry applications, and structural concrete drainage channels. Because these fixes are geographically disparate and serve as temporary holding measures, execution must balance cost-efficiency against structural durability.

Assessment: In-House vs. Outsourced Execution

Evaluating these multi-location holding actions reveals distinct trade-offs between utilizing internal estate resources and procuring commercial contractors:

  • The Estate's In-House Capacity & Past Learnings: The estate already possesses an experienced internal team equipped with the necessary machinery to execute slurrying operations directly. Historically, past slurrying efforts experienced premature failure not due to equipment or technical skill deficits, but rather because of deficient ongoing maintenance programmes and a lack of rigorous operational supervision.
  • In-House Strengths (Agility & Drainage): Highly responsive for emergency repairs (such as clearing silted drains and basic cold-tar shoveling) while avoiding steep commercial mobilization and travel fees across disparate locations. Furthermore, minor concrete trenching and stormwater diversion channels are handled internally. Rationale for In-House Trenching: Manual excavation and small-batch concrete casting for localized drainage can be scheduled flexibly around seasonal rainfall observations without incurring minimum-billing call-out fees from heavy civil contractors.
  • The Case for Outsourced Heavy Stabilization: Heavy layerworks stabilization requiring specialized heavy equipment (such as vibrating rollers and industrial mechanical mixers) remains allocated to commercial contractors to guarantee structural sub-base integrity.
  • Recommended Strategy (Enforced In-House Slurrying with Strict Supervision): Leverage the estate's existing slurrying equipment and experienced team internally to maximize cost savings, provided that leadership implements robust ongoing maintenance scheduling and strict field supervision frameworks to safeguard application quality.
