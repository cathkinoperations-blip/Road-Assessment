Appendix A1: Structural Sub-Base Strategy

Technical Reference — Base Work Scope

A critical distinction in the master assessment plan is separating surface-level waterproofing from full structural sub-base reconstruction. Surface treatments do not automatically require redoing the sub-base, preserving capital where structural integrity remains intact[cite: 10].

1. Surface Sealing Only

Sub-base Left Intact

  • Mechanism: Applied directly onto existing asphalt[cite: 10].
  • Purpose: Waterproofs the surface, seals micro-cracks, and prevents binder oxidation[cite: 10].
  • Applicable Conditions: Fine map cracking, surface weathering, pinhole loss[cite: 10].
  • Target Corridors: Duiker Ridge, Village 4 & 5, Bridge 3 & 7 approaches[cite: 10].

2. Targeted Box Cuts

Localized Base Repairs

  • Mechanism: Cold-milling or hand-excavating localized failure zones down to sub-base[cite: 10].
  • Purpose: Rebuilds isolated sub-base collapse before applying a continuous surface seal[cite: 10].
  • Applicable Conditions: Wheel-path alligator cracking, isolated depressions, sunken patches[cite: 10].
  • Target Corridors: Bridge 5, Eland Way, Oribi Lane[cite: 10].

3. Full Structural Rebuild

Full Depth Excavation / Paver Upgrade

  • Mechanism: Total removal of failed sub-base; installation of concrete edge beams, drainage, or pavers[cite: 10].
  • Purpose: Re-establishes structural load-bearing capacity, containment, and long-term durability[cite: 10].
  • Applicable Conditions: Severe washout trenches, edge scour, full corridor oxidation, high turning shear[cite: 10].
  • Target Corridors: Champagne Ridge (280m), Bridge 6 & 8 washout zones, Arum Lane apron[cite: 10].

Appendix A2: Bituminous Material Comparison

Technical Reference — Cape Seal vs. Slurry Overlay
Parameter Slurry Overlay (Slurry Seal)[cite: 10] Cape Seal[cite: 10]
System Construction Single-layer pre-mixture of bitumen emulsion, fine aggregate (sand/small stone), water, and cement filler[cite: 10]. Two-step hybrid treatment: Single chip seal (coarse stone on emulsion) topped with a slurry seal layer[cite: 10].
Layer Thickness 3 mm to 6 mm (Thin surfacing)[cite: 10] 10 mm to 15 mm (Medium-thick matrix)[cite: 10]
Structural Strength Relies on binder/fine aggregate density; lower shear resistance under heavy turning[cite: 10]. High tensile strength provided by embedded stone skeleton matrix; excellent resistance to turning shear[cite: 10].
Surface Texture & Skid Resistance Smooth, uniform, sand-like finish. Extremely quiet under traffic; standard traction[cite: 10]. Macro-textured skid resistance of a chip seal combined with the smooth, flexible finish of slurry (no loose stones)[cite: 10].
Expected Lifespan 5 to 7 Years[cite: 10] 10 to 15 Years[cite: 10]
Ideal Estate Usage Low-volume straight runs, light residential streets, preventative binder sealing (e.g., Duiker Ridge, Bridge 3 & 7)[cite: 10]. High-wear corridors, steep inclines, sweeping curves, and oxidized main arteries (e.g., Champagne Ridge, Oribi Lane)[cite: 10].

Technical Reference — TRH14 / COLTO Standards

Standard South African pavement material classifications according to TRH14/COLTO specifications. Structural selection dictates load-bearing capacity, shear strength, and compaction requirements for estate road sub-bases[cite: 10].

G1[cite: 10] Dense-graded crushed stone (High Shear Strength)[cite: 10] 100% crushed rock; Parent rock UCS > 120 MPa; Compacted to 86–88% Apparent Relative Density (ARD)[cite: 10]. Heavy-duty base courses subject to extreme wheel loads and high traffic volumes[cite: 10].
G2[cite: 10] Dense-graded crushed stone (Standard Structural Base)[cite: 10] Crushed gravel/rock; Max aggregate size 37.5mm; Compacted to 100–102% Mod AASHTO density[cite: 10]. Primary structural base layer for high-wear estate corridors, paver bed box-cuts, and junction aprons[cite: 10].
G3[cite: 10] Crushed stone / Selected natural gravel mix[cite: 10] At least 50% crushed particles; Compacted to 98–100% Mod AASHTO; CBR > 80%[cite: 10]. Base layer for light-to-medium traffic roads or sub-base for heavy loading[cite: 10].
G4[cite: 10] Natural gravel (High Quality)[cite: 10] Maximum PI < 6; CBR ≥ 80% at 98% Mod AASHTO compaction; Max aggregate size 53mm[cite: 10]. Standard upper sub-base layer under G2/G3 base courses or paving blocks[cite: 10].
G5[cite: 10] Natural gravel (Medium Quality)[cite: 10] Maximum PI < 10; CBR ≥ 45% at 95% Mod AASHTO compaction[cite: 10]. Lower sub-base layer; washout excavation backfill and general sub-base stabilization[cite: 10].

Appendix A5: Regional Benchmarking — Cathkin Estates vs. Amber Valley

Comparative Analysis — Remote Logistics & Operational Models

Objective

To determine the optimal road surface material strategy for Cathkin Estates by evaluating structural requirements, remote logistics, and operational sustainability relative to regional benchmarks.

The Challenge: Remote Mountain Logistics

Unlike urban-adjacent developments (e.g., Amber Valley in Howick) that benefit from proximity to centralized ready-mix batching plants and dense contractor networks, Cathkin Estates faces:

  • Logistical Constraints: High transit premiums and time-sensitive delivery windows for ready-mix concrete in remote areas.
  • Climatic Vulnerability: High-altitude weather patterns impacting concrete curing, increasing the risk of structural failure during construction.
  • Operational Autonomy: The need for maintenance solutions that allow the estate to remain self-sufficient in repairing underground services and local subsidence.
