Technical Evaluation & Engineering Assessment

  • Kerb Foundation Spec: The proposal specifies a 50mm thick concrete foundation for Fig 10 Kerbs. Engineering review should verify if 50mm provides adequate structural support against vehicular loads, as standard specifications often call for a minimum of 75mm–100mm unreinforced concrete haunching/base.
  • Paver Structural Profile: 80mm G-Block pavers are specified, which are well-suited for medium-to-heavy vehicular traffic expected on estate roads.
  • Bedding & Jointing: 20mm sand bedding with sand grouting is standard, though long-term performance will depend heavily on sub-grade compaction (not explicitly detailed in the scope).
  • Measurement Methodology: Note 4 dictates that areas will be measured inclusive of wastage due to edge cutting. This is standard practice but increases the unit cost exposure per net square meter laid.

Commercial & Contractual Terms Assessment

  • Price Vulnerability: Note 1 subjects all prices to fluctuating fuel prices with no explicit cap, validity period, or formula provided, creating budget uncertainty.
  • Scope Creep Risk: Note 2 dictates that any "remedial work" must be priced separately prior to commencement. Stakeholders should audit site conditions beforehand to define baseline sub-grade readiness and avoid surprise variation orders.
  • Quantity Risk: Note 3 establishes that all areas are subject to re-measurement upon completion, meaning final project cost may vary from the current quotation.

Key Exclusions & Fine Print Caveats

  • Prices tied to current fuel pricing with an open-ended escalation clause.
  • Remedial/prep work excluded from current baseline and subject to separate ad-hoc pricing.
  • Final billing is strictly subject to re-measurement on completion.
  • Wastage for edge-cutting is billed directly into the measured areas.

Discrepancies & Audit Findings

  • Lack of Terms: The quote completely omits standard commercial clauses such as payment milestones, retention percentages, project timeline/duration, and penalty clauses for late delivery.
  • Geotechnical Risk: No mention of sub-base stabilization or compaction testing certificates, leaving potential exposure to structural failure if existing road bases are sub-standard.
