Technical Evaluation & Engineering Assessment

  • Asphalt Substrate Overlay Methodology: Paving directly over the existing asphalt base avoids costly excavation and sub-base reconstruction. However, because asphalt acts as an impermeable barrier, bedding sand moisture can become trapped above the asphalt layer if weep holes or adequate lateral drainage channels are not integrated along the lower kerb edges.
  • Kerb Foundation & Depth Alignment: The proposal specifies a 50mm thick concrete foundation for Fig 10 Kerbs. Because kerbs are installed alongside an existing asphalt deck, the installation requires asphalt edge sawing or removal to seat the kerb footings at the correct finished road height. Engineering review should confirm if 50mm concrete thickness provides sufficient restraint against lateral movement under wheel loads.
  • Paver Structural Profile: 80mm G-Block pavers are specified. Combined with the underlying structural capacity of the existing asphalt layer, this composite structure provides substantial load distribution for heavy traffic, provided the existing asphalt is free from deep structural rutting or sub-grade failure.
  • Bedding & Jointing Sand Layer: A 20mm bedding sand layer directly on asphalt acts as a leveling cushion. The thickness must remain uniform (15mm–25mm); severe dips or potholes in the existing asphalt must be patched prior to sand bedding to prevent differential paver settlement.
  • Measurement Methodology: Note 4 dictates that areas will be measured inclusive of wastage due to edge cutting. This is standard practice but increases the material quantity exposure per net square meter laid.

Execution & Technical Terms Assessment

  • Existing Surface Preparation: Placing pavers over asphalt requires thorough sweeping, removal of organic matter, and filling of severe depressions or alligator cracks prior to bedding sand placement to ensure a stable sub-bedding surface.
  • Scope Boundaries & Remedial Work: Note 2 dictates that any "remedial work" (e.g., asphalt leveling, deep pothole repairs, or edge milling) must be priced separately prior to commencement. Engineers should audit the existing asphalt surface conditions beforehand to establish clear prep-work boundaries.
  • Quantity Verification: Note 3 establishes that all areas are subject to re-measurement upon completion, meaning final quantities may vary from the original estimate.

Key Technical Exclusions & Scope Caveats

  • Pre-paving asphalt repairs, deep hole patching, or surface milling excluded from current baseline and subject to separate ad-hoc scope definition.
  • Perimeter edge keying / asphalt sawing for level transitions at tie-ins is not explicitly broken out.
  • Final billing subject strictly to re-measurement of installed areas on completion.
  • Wastage for edge-cutting is included directly into the measured areas.

Engineering & Audit Findings

  • Subgrade/Asphalt Integrity Risk: Laying pavers directly over existing asphalt assumes the underlying asphalt layer and basecourse are structurally sound. If the underlying asphalt is suffering from active sub-grade moisture degradation, overlaying pavers will not prevent continued sub-base settlement.
  • Drainage & Moisture Trapping: Water infiltrating through paver joints will collect on top of the asphalt layer. Without deliberate subsurface drainage release points at kerbs or low points, saturated bedding sand can cause paver instability or bed washouts under heavy traffic.
  • Omission of Execution Parameters: The quote omits project timeline/duration, quality compliance milestones, and formal surface handover specifications for the asphalt base before paving commences.
