Engineering Review Questionnaire

Technical & Operational Verification Questions for Consulting Engineer — Cathkin Estates Road Upgrades

Review Context: Based on the July 2026 Master Infrastructure Assessment and procurement evaluations, the HOA is proposing a hybrid execution model: contracted vendor implementation for Tier 1 high-shear zones (including Paver overlay by Raciti on Champagne Ridge) and targeted in-house holding fixes/maintenance on secondary corridors. All designs and pavement layerworks must explicitly accommodate ongoing estate construction traffic limited to a maximum Gross Vehicle Mass (GVM) of 10 tonnes.

1. Vendor Scope & Asphalt Overlay Methodology Vendor Review

Focus: Raciti's proposal to lay 80mm G-Block pavers directly over existing asphalt (e.g., Champagne Ridge / 810 m²) under 10t GVM vehicle loading.
  • 1.1 Sub-Base Integrity & Structural Load Capacity Under 10t GVM Traffic
    Does the existing oxidized asphalt substrate on Champagne Ridge provide adequate structural capacity (in terms of Equivalent Standard Axles / E80s) to support an 80mm paver overlay under continuous 10t GVM construction traffic, to what extent is localized G2/G5 sub-base undercutting required prior to paving?
    Context: Paving directly over asphalt saves excavation costs, but site evidence shows severe binder oxidation, patch failures, and edge breakouts on Champagne Ridge. Under repeated 10t axle loads, a compromised sub-base will lead to rapid sub-grade deformation, bedding sand pumping, and surface rutting.
  • 1.2 Subsurface Drainage & Bedding Liquefaction Under Heavy Loading
    How should subsurface drainage and weep channels be specified along lower kerb edges to prevent water accumulation in the 20mm bedding sand layer, which could otherwise liquefy and pump under repetitive 10t wheel loads?
    Context: Infiltration through paver joints will pool on top of the impermeable asphalt. Without weep channels or lateral drainage outlets, water trapped in the sand bedding under heavy 10t vehicular action will cause joint sand loss, paver movement, and structural failure.
  • 1.3 Surface Runoff & Cross-Fall Capture (Kerb Inlet & Channel Capacity)
    On steep inclines (e.g., Champagne Ridge), are standard Fig 10 kerbs and side channels hydraulically sized to capture peak storm intensity without overtopping kerb edges and scouring downstream road shoulders or embankment fills?
    Context: High-velocity surface runoff on steep gradients can easily overtop standard side channels if not paired with properly spaced kerb inlets, leading to rapid shoulder erosion and loss of lateral support.
  • 1.4 Sub-Base Transverse Interceptor Drains on Steep Gradients
    For long continuous steep gradients (e.g., Champagne Ridge), should transverse sub-surface interceptor drains (cutoff drains) be installed across the width of the roadway at regular intervals to prevent water from migrating longitudinally within the bedding layer or sub-base?
    Context: On steep slopes, water infiltrating paver joints tends to flow downhill underneath the paving blocks rather than draining sideways, accumulating hydrostatic pressure at the bottom of the incline and driving subsurface erosion.
  • 1.5 Concrete Kerb Footing & Lateral Containment under Heavy Turning Forces
    Is the specified 50mm concrete foundation under the Fig 10 kerbs structurally sufficient to resist the severe lateral thrust and shear stresses generated by loaded 10t construction vehicles braking or turning on steep inclines?
    Context: Raciti's quote specifies laying Fig 10 kerbs on a 50mm concrete bed. Heavy 10t construction traffic on steep gradients generates strong horizontal shear forces that could displace kerbs if edge backing and concrete footings are undersized.
  • 1.6 Surface Leveling & Pre-Paving Remedial Tolerances
    What maximum deformation or rutting tolerance should be permitted on the existing asphalt deck before localized asphalt leveling or cold-patching is mandated to prevent point-loading failures under 10t vehicles?
    Context: The vendor specification assumes a uniform 20mm bedding sand layer (15mm–25mm range). Irregularities or depressions in the underlying asphalt under heavy 10t axle weight will lead to uneven bedding compaction and differential paver settlement.

2. In-House Execution & Holding Repair Specifications In-House Scope

Focus: In-house low-TCO holding repairs across Tier 3 & Tier 4 corridors (~901 m scope) subjected to estate traffic up to 10t GVM.
  • 2.1 Crack Sealing Material Selection & Elasticity Under Heavy Wheel Loads
    For in-house holding repairs (e.g., Eland Way, Dierama Lane, Whydah Way), what elastomeric hot-pour crack seal or cold-applied polymer specifications are required to withstand high pavement deflection caused by 10t construction traffic?
    Context: In-house teams will handle crack sealing across several long corridors (e.g., 90m downhill split on Eland Way). Under 10t vehicle wheel loads, flexible crack sealants are essential to prevent premature fatigue debonding and water ingress.
  • 2.2 Bitumen Mastic & Cold-Mix Patching Durability Under 10t Axles
    What are the recommended application guidelines, layer thickness limits, and compaction targets for bitumen mastic and cold-mix asphalt patching to prevent shoving and shear distortion under 10t GVM loads (e.g., Oribi Lane, Village 5)?
    Context: In-house repairs on Oribi Lane (~155m) and Village 5 (~16m) require spot leveling and over-band sealing. Cold-mix patches can rapidly deform under heavy 10t construction axles if not properly specified and compacted.
  • 2.3 Granular Base Selection & Compaction Density for In-House Box Cuts
    Where localized square-cut patch excavations are conducted internally (e.g., Whydah Way, Bridge 5), is G2 dense-graded crushed stone compacted to 98% Mod AASHTO mandatory to support 10t GVM loads, or is standard G4/G5 gravel sufficient?
    Context: Appendix A1 and A3 outline G1–G10 granular standards. Confirming the minimum aggregate quality and compaction requirements for internal box-cut repairs ensures patches do not fail prematurely under heavy estate construction traffic.
  • 2.4 Scour & Edge Protection at Paved-to-Unpaved Material Transitions
    What specific edge beam, concrete dish-lining, or rip-rap apron details should be specified at transitions between newly paved zones and gravel or unlined secondary roads to prevent sub-cutting and edge collapse during heavy stormwater flows?
    Context: Abrupt transitions between paved surfaces and unpaved secondary roads are highly vulnerable to localized scour and undercut as water accelerates off paved sections onto softer material.

3. High-Risk Critical Zones & Subgrade Stabilization Critical Infrastructure

Focus: Tier 1 & Tier 2 critical washout zones, bridge approaches, and dam embankments bearing 10t GVM loads.
  • 3.1 Bridge Washout & Headwall Stability Under 10t Vehicle Dynamic Loads
    Do the proposed rip-rap, backfill compaction (G5/G2), and headwall shoulder stabilization measures at Bridge 6 and Bridge 8 provide adequate structural capacity and lateral earth pressure resistance against dynamic impact from passing 10t vehicles?
    Context: Bridge 6 exhibits edge scour along parapet wall footings, and Bridge 8 has headwall shoulder collapses and 59m incline fractures. Heavy construction loads passing near compromised bridge edges pose dynamic collapse and safety risks.
  • 3.2 Cross-Drainage & Culvert Capacity for Peak Storm Events
    Do existing pipe and box culverts along secondary and primary corridors have adequate hydraulic capacity (e.g., 1-in-20 year storm return period) to prevent headwater ponding, road overtopping, and sub-base saturation under heavy summer rainstorms?
    Context: Inadequate culvert sizing causes storm runoff to back up behind embankments or overtop road decks, leading to severe edge washout, piping, and pavement undermining.
  • 3.3 Energy Dissipation & Outfall Erosion Control
    Where stormwater chutes and mitre drains discharge high-velocity runoff off high-grade road sections, what energy dissipation measures (e.g., gabion mattresses, reno mattresses, or stone headwall aprons) are required to prevent deep gully erosion on adjoining estate turf and natural slopes?
    Context: Concentrated water discharged from road channels onto unlined natural terrain causes rapid down-cutting and gully erosion, threatening nearby road subgrades and environmental stability.
  • 3.4 Cathkin Drive Dam Series Embankment Load Bearing & Piping Risk
    Is the inclusion of agricultural sub-soil drainage lines together with G2 sub-base reconstruction sufficient to stabilize the Cathkin Drive Dam embankment against asphalt shoving and sub-grade shear under repeat 10t construction traffic?
    Context: The dam series (~103m) exhibits embankment slumping, spillway scour, and asphalt shoving on the 28m deck. Heavy 10t vehicle passage accelerates sub-grade shear in moisture-saturated embankments.

4. Quality Assurance, Governance & Plan Execution Governance & Sign-Off

Focus: Oversight, material testing standards for 10t GVM rating, handover protocols, and schedule/budget risk mitigation.
  • 4.1 Quality Control Testing & Paver Crushing Strength Certification
    What mandatory site testing protocols—specifically SANS 1058 class 40/2.6 (min 40MPa crushing strength) for 80mm pavers and Mod AASHTO density testing for sub-base layers—should be mandated prior to approving vendor payments for 10t heavy-traffic corridors?
    Context: Vendor documentation omits specific quality testing milestones. Standard residential pavers (30MPa) will crack and spall under 10t GVM loads; high-strength 40MPa/80mm Class 1 pavers are required for commercial/construction traffic.
  • 4.2 Measurement & Scope Gap Verification
    How should the HOA structure the site re-measurement process (accounting for edge-cutting waste allowances) to prevent scope creep or unexpected cost variations during final billing?
    Context: Vendor terms state that final billing will be subject to complete re-measurement on completion, inclusive of edge cutting wastage.
  • 4.3 Workmanship Guarantees, Material Warranties & Defects Liability
    Is the proposed 12-month contractor workmanship guarantee alongside standard supplier material certificates sufficient for civil roadworks carrying 10t traffic, or should the HOA negotiate a standard 12-month Defects Notification Period (DNP) backed by a retention holdback (e.g., 5–10%)?
    Context: The vendor is proposing a 12-month workmanship guarantee and supplier certificates. Engineering guidance is needed to ensure post-handover issues—such as sub-grade settlement, edge kerb displacement, or joint sand loss under 10t loads—are contractually enforceable against the contractor.
  • 4.4 Construction Sequencing & Rainy Season Risk Mitigation
    Given that Task 0.0 defines November through February as the rainy season, is it technically viable to execute holding repairs (slurry/emulsion in Village 2 & 3) and stormwater trenching during November–December, or should these earthworks/sealing tasks be fully completed prior to November 1st?
    Context: In-house holding repairs (in Nov) and stormwater U-drain trenching (Nov–Dec) overlap with the rainy season window. Unconsolidated trench backfill under wet conditions easily washes out or collapses under 10t construction traffic.
  • 4.5 Compression of Tier 1 Paving Schedule (Champagne Ridge)
    What critical path milestones (e.g., sub-grade compaction approval, paver delivery, site establishment) must be met to ensure the 280m Champagne Ridge upgrade (R950k) can be completed within October 2026 without risking incomplete works going into the rainy season?
    Context: Champagne Ridge is scheduled entirely in October 2026 but this schedule is slipping due to rain, reviews and contracting delays.
  • 4.6 Interim Protection for Postponed Bridge Works (Bridges 6, 8 & Cathkin Dam)
    For major critical zones scheduled for post-rainy season execution (e.g., Bridges 6 & 8 in March 2027, Cathkin Dam in May 2027), what temporary protection measures (e.g., heavy sandbagging, temporary chute diversions, 10t load speed/margin restrictions) should be deployed before November 2026?
    Context: High-shear bridge approaches and dam embankments will remain exposed through the 4-month wet season before permanent paving occurs in Q1/Q2 2027. Interim mitigation prevents escalating repair costs.
