Cathkin Estates Road Rehabilitation Program

Executive Program Summary & Commercial Due Diligence Report for Homeowners
Phase 1: Diagnosis & Strategic Decision

1. Why We Shifted from Tar Patching to Interlocking Pavers

Following extensive field surveys across 1,595m of estate roads, we evaluated various surface alternatives—including hot-mix asphalt (670m coverage per R1M), chip & spray (850m–1,100m coverage), and reinforced concrete (450m coverage)—and concluded that continuous asphalt patching on high-wear inclines was failing due to thermal degradation and stormwater erosion. To protect long-term property values, a strategic decision was proposed to transition high-impact corridors to heavy-duty interlocking pavers (610m coverage), offering a substantially lower 20-year Total Cost of Ownership when considering capital outlay, ongoing maintenance (cost, frequency, and complexity).

Supporting Technical Data: Interactive GIS network maps, corridor tier classifications, and the strategic TCO presentation.

Phase 2: Technical Scope & Maintenance

2. Program Scope: Rebuilds vs. Holding Fixes

To optimise spending, the road network was divided into two distinct operational scopes. Permanent heavy-duty paving (~694m) was allocated to critical failure points like Champagne Ridge and Bridges 6 & 8. Meanwhile, lower-wear residential lanes (~901m) were designated for cost-effective holding fixes (cold-mix patching, crack sealing, and concrete U-drain trenching).

Supporting Engineering Specifications: COLTO/TRH14 aggregate sub-base standards and field inspection photographic logs.

Phase 3: Financial Framework & Governance

3. Funding Model: Zero Special Levy Mandate

The entire R2.85M infrastructure roadmap spanning FY 25/26 and FY 26/27 has been structured without raising a special levy on homeowners. Funding is fully absorbed through planned Capex Reserve Fund drawdowns by deferring non-urgent capital projects totaling R1.465M—including the Landini tractor (R400k), 18km electric fence upgrades (R300k), park development (R300k), and solar off-grid allocations (R120k)—allowing the estate to prioritize critical road rehabilitation without impacting homeowner cash flows. A 4-month weather buffer has also been built into the multi-year Gantt schedule to prevent costly rain delays during peak Drakensberg wet seasons.

Phase 4: Commercial Execution & Due Diligence

4. Vendor Selection & Tender Evaluation (Champagne Ridge — Scope Normalization)

An objective, multi-criteria evaluation framework was established to assess proposals. Vendors were scored across commercial pricing (70%), technical compliance and turn-key execution (5%), operational risk and logistics (5%), warranty structures and accountability (10%), and reputation and track record (10%). Raciti emerged as the top-scoring bidder (8.8 / 10.0) with the lowest normalized cost of ZAR 743,762 (incl. VAT), securing the recommendation for award subject to fixed-price terms for sub-base and kerbing.
